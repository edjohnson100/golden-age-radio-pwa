Golden Age Radio is a little listening room for public-domain Old Time Radio — the comedy, detective, horror, western, drama, holiday music, big band, science-fiction, and history broadcasts America gathered around the dial to hear in the 1930s–50s. The shows stream free from the Internet Archive. No account, no subscription, no nickel required.

The Living Room Dial

For the living room. A walnut mantel clock-radio with a live clock, a glowing magic-eye tuner and a red dial needle. Turn the tuning knob, or tap a station preset, and settle in.

  • Tune by frequency — 600 to 1580 KC
  • Live analog clock built into the dial
  • Power switch, volume & tuning knobs

The Diner Selector

For the diner booth. A chrome arch-top jukebox with neon, a spinning record and red-and-cream title strips. Pick a strip — A1, C2, G1 — and drop the needle.

  • Pick by title strip — A to I, 1 to 6+
  • Spinning vinyl & tonearm that swings in
  • Chrome transport controls, free play

Pick a player above — Mantel Radio or Jukebox. They play the same shows, so pick whichever machine you like looking at.

Same underneath — by design

The two players look and feel different on purpose. Underneath, they are the same radio. Nothing is exclusive to one machine: switch any time and you’ll find the same shows, the same episodes, in the same order.

How to listen

1.

Pick a machine

Mantel radio for tuning, jukebox for picking strips. Either one — they play the same thing.

2.

Pick a station

Tap any of the 9 stations across the dial or rack, or open Browse Catalog and search by show, title or year.

3.

Press play

Press Tune In / Play. Use Prev / Next to move through episodes, Shuffle to mix them up, and the seek bar to jump ahead.

Good to know: the 41 starter episodes play right away. While you listen, each player quietly loads the full catalog from the Internet Archive — hundreds more episodes from the same 22 collections — so Browse Catalog keeps growing. Shuffle never splits a multi-part story. It works on a phone, a tablet or a computer, with nothing to install.
